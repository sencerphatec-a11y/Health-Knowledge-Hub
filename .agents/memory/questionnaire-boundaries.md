---
name: Questionnaire boundaries
description: Privacy, clinical-claim limits, and reproduction-rights decisions for health self-assessments.
---

Health self-assessment answers and scores should remain private, transient browser state by default rather than becoming saved medical histories or analytics events.

**Why:** These tools can contain sensitive mental-health, family-history, and symptom information. The user requested educational questionnaires and results, not storage or transmission of health responses.

**How to apply:** Do not add answer-bearing URLs, persistence, analytics, or sharing without a separate explicit requirement and a clear privacy/consent design. Keep normal browsing and opening educational resources separate from disclosing answers.

Keep validated symptom scales distinct from original educational reflections; an organizational score or count of topics is not a disease probability, suicide-risk determination, diagnosis, or exercise clearance.

**Why:** The user requested both standardized mental-health screeners and custom wellness/lifestyle feedback. Extending validation claims from one instrument to another would mislead readers.

**How to apply:** Preserve a standardized tool’s published questions, recall period, response values, and scoring. Explain bespoke scoring transparently and do not borrow clinical risk labels or endorsement claims. Refer clinical decisions to qualified professionals.

Verify reproduction and electronic-embedding rights before implementing any additional named clinical questionnaire. Public access alone does not imply permission to embed or adapt it.

**Why:** When checked on 2026-10-09, the PHQ/GAD instruction manual permitted reproduction, while the official PAR-Q+ Collaboration required written consent for electronic surveys and derivative materials. See https://eparmedx.com/print-versions-of-par-q/ and https://eparmedx.com/how-to-cite.

**How to apply:** Recheck authoritative terms before changing the exercise tool or adding new instruments. Link to an official restricted instrument rather than renaming an unlicensed adaptation as the official tool. Any independently written reflection must not claim the original instrument’s validation.
